## The Three Headaches of Shopify Product Images

Shopify has clear performance recommendations for product images, but most sellers ignore them. The result: slow loading, low PageSpeed scores, and lost conversions.

### 1. Massive File Sizes

Shopify recommends product images under 200KB, thumbnails under 50KB. Yet most product photos come straight from a camera or phone — 2-5MB each is common.

A 50-product store: if each original is 3MB, loading 10 product images on the homepage is 30MB. On mobile, that's theoretically a 30-second wait. In reality, shoppers leave after 3 seconds.

### 2. Inconsistent Dimensions

Product photos from different sources — 2000x3000, 4000x3000, 800x1200. Shopify themes expect uniform aspect ratios in thumbnail grids. Mismatched sizes mean either cropped products (parts cut off) or broken layouts.

### 3. Terrible Filenames

`IMG_3829.jpg`, `DSC_0001.jpg`, `photo_001.jpg` — these filenames mean nothing to Google. Meanwhile, `wool-coat-grey-size-m.webp` is an SEO asset. Google Images and Google Shopping use filenames to understand image content and match them to buyer queries.

## The TinyOpt All-in-One Workflow

### Step 1: Batch Compress (the core)

Open TinyOpt, drag all 250 product images into the 'Compress' tab:

1. **Output format**: WebP (60-70% smaller than JPEG, visually lossless)
2. **Resize mode**: Fit, 2048x2048 (Shopify's recommended max product image size)
3. **Output folder**: Choose a new folder to preserve originals
4. **Click Start**: Multi-key concurrency processes 250 images in ~3 minutes

Results: 2-5MB originals → 80-180KB WebP, 90%+ size reduction, indistinguishable from originals to the naked eye.

### Step 2: Batch Rename (SEO)

Switch to the 'Rename' tab:

1. Template: `{name}-{index}`
2. Pre-name files by product folder
3. Use `{index}` for auto-numbering: `wool-coat-grey-01.webp`

SEO-friendly filenames help Google understand your product images. A shopper searching for "grey wool coat" is far more likely to see your product in Google Images or Google Shopping results.

### Step 3: Upload to Shopify

Compressed WebP files upload fast. Two things to remember:

1. **Shopify supports WebP natively**: Upload WebP directly — Shopify handles browser compatibility automatically (falls back to JPEG for unsupported browsers)
2. **Alt text**: After upload, add Alt tags in Shopify admin for each product image — your second line of defense for product image SEO

## Pro Tips

1. **Keep originals**: Compression is lossy and irreversible. Always keep a backup of raw files.
2. **Stack API keys**: TinyPNG free tier gives 500 compressions/month per key. Add multiple keys in TinyOpt for automatic rotation.
3. **Default to WebP**: Unless a third-party platform requires JPEG, WebP is the right default for e-commerce.
4. **Use crop mode**: If your Shopify theme requires 1:1 square product images, bulk-crop to 1:1 in one click.
