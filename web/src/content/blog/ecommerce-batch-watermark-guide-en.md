## Why E-Commerce Sellers Need Watermarks

### Pain 1: Etsy Image Theft Is Rampant

Etsy markets itself as a handmade, original goods platform. But that very emphasis on originality makes image theft rampant. A hit bracelet's product photo can be copied and pasted to 20+ stores within 48 hours. No watermark means no lock on your door.

### Pain 2: Brand Asset Leakage

You paid a photographer hundreds for product photos. Competitors use them for free. Your photo goes viral on Pinterest with thousands of repins — but without a watermark, nobody knows it came from your store. Every product image is a brand asset. A watermark is your logo.

### Pain 3: Manual Processing Is Too Expensive

Open Photoshop, create a text layer, adjust opacity, export... 10 photos takes an hour. What if you have 200 product images?

## Which Watermark Works Best?

| Type | Pro | Con | Best For |
|------|-----|-----|----------|
| Corner text | Minimal product obstruction | Easy to crop out | Brand exposure, worried about conversion |
| Diagonal semi-transparent text | Hard to crop, strong protection | Partial product obstruction | Etsy originals, high-ticket items |
| Logo + text combo | High brand recognition | Requires design effort | Branded independent store owners |
| Full-screen tiled | Nearly impossible to remove | Ruins visuals, kills conversion | Internal previews only, never for customers |

My recommendation: **Diagonal semi-transparent text + small corner logo**, balancing protection and experience. 35-45% opacity — visible enough to identify the brand, subtle enough to not kill purchase intent.

## The Golden Rule

> A watermark tells shoppers "this is my product" — not "I don't trust you to look at my product."

If the watermark is so large it hides product details, shoppers close the tab. If it's so small a quick crop removes it, you wasted your time. Find that sweet spot — that's the advantage of TinyOpt's visual drag-and-drop: see the final result before you process.
